Tiering follows CBRE's Global Data Center Trends classification (primary = the seven largest US wholesale markets; secondary = mature smaller markets; emerging = recently-active build-out markets). Market-level MW figures are illustrative seed values rounded to published industry ranges; the Facilities layer seeds 30+ named buildings with operator, developer, and funding source. Refresh via POST /api/data-centers/refresh. Spec ratio = under-construction MW × (1 − pre-leased %) ÷ built MW; pipeline ratio = (UC + planned) ÷ built; concentration = market share of national built MW. Funding-source taxonomy: hyperscaler self-fund, public REIT, infra fund / PE, sovereign/strategic JV (e.g. Stargate), specialty/venture, public specialty.

Stranded-asset risk (0–100) = tenant concentration (0–30) + tenant credit quality (0–20) + speculative build component (0–15) + funding-source resilience (0–10) + geographic correlation (0–10) + tenant balance-sheet stretch (committed AI capex vs. tenant FCF, 0–15). At-risk MW applies a default-probability matrix on (tenant tier × status × scenario). The scenario selector at the top of the page swaps in three calibrations: Mild (capex normalizes), Moderate (demand neutralizes), Severe (AI demand crash). Calibration is illustrative — substitute house probabilities by editing _SCENARIOS and TENANT_FCF_HEADROOM in backend/data_centers/service.py.

Methodology & sources

Market-level MW (operational, under-construction, pre-leased %, vacancy) for the seven CBRE primary markets (NoVA, DFW, Phoenix, Atlanta, Chicago, Silicon Valley, NY-NJ) plus selected secondaries (Houston, Austin, Hillsboro) come from the CBRE North America Data Center Trends H1 2025 report and CBRE press releases. Headline figures: primary-market inventory hit a record 8,155 MW (+43% YoY), 74.3% of all under-construction capacity is pre-leased, primary-market vacancy is at a record-low 1.6% (NoVA colo as low as 0.72%). Secondary and emerging markets outside CBRE's primary set are estimates derived from utility-queue filings and state economic-development announcements.

Facility-level MW, tenant, developer, and funding source for the 33 named buildings come from primary sources: company press releases (Microsoft, Meta, Google, AWS, Apple, Oracle), Crusoe newsroom, SEC filings (EQIX, DLR, IRM 10-Ks), DigitalBridge / Macquarie / Blackstone / KKR / Brookfield holding disclosures, and trade press (Data Center Dynamics, Data Center Frontier, Tom's Hardware, Bloomberg, Reuters). Each row in the Top stranded-asset risks table carries a ↗ link to the primary source. Confidence dots ( high / medium / low) reflect whether the MW figure is directly disclosed (high), inferred from utility filings or building permits (medium), or a campus-design / typical-Meta-hall estimate (low). Hyperscalers (Meta, Google, Apple) rarely disclose facility MW directly; figures are inferred from state filings and trackers like interconnection.fyi and Baxtel.

Tenant credit profile uses public S&P / Moody's ratings for IG mega-caps (Microsoft / Apple AAA · Google AA+ · Amazon AA · Meta A+ · Oracle BBB+) and implied private-market ratings for OpenAI (B-) and xAI (CCC) reflecting their cash-burn / capital-raise dependence. Spread (bps) = annual PD × LGD with LGD ≈ 60% on lease obligations.

The stranded-asset risk score, writedown probability matrices, and stress-test predicates are house assumptions, not pulled from a third-party feed. They live in backend/data_centers/service.py as _SCENARIOS, _STRESS_TESTS, _WRITEDOWN_PROB, and TENANT_FCF_HEADROOM and are easy to swap for a house view.
